# How businesses usually set up Attio

## Work out the sales motion

Infer it if there's enough evidence. Otherwise ask the user.

| Motion      | Tells                                                         | What it means for the model                                                                                                               |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Product-led | A "Get started" button, a free plan, usage-based pricing      | Enable Users and Workspaces. Champion is a link to People.                                                                                |
| Sales-led   | "Request a demo", no public prices, enterprise customer logos | Companies, People and Deals. Often Products, Line items, Contracts or Invoices as custom objects, if the team works them in Attio.        |
| Channel     | A partner program, reseller or MSP language                   | A partner organization is a company with a tier select. Partner deals and market development fund requests can justify their own objects. |
| Marketplace | Separate pages for the supply and demand sides                | Deals for partnerships with businesses. A Matching object to pair the two sides, only if matching happens in Attio, not another tool.     |
| Hybrid      | A mix                                                         | Commit to one main motion, add what the others need on top, and don't model each one in full.                                             |

## How similar businesses set it up

These are reference points, not rules. Use them to check a draft, or to tell the user "this is how businesses like yours usually set it up", then adapt to what they tell you.

- **Sales-led software or services:** People, Companies and Deals, often with Products and Line items, and sometimes Contracts or Invoices.
- **Software with product data:** adds Users and Workspaces, often with a Customer Success list on Workspaces.
- **Agencies:** similar to sales-led. Clients stay as companies, and a Projects object is common.
- **Investors, such as VC:** the widest range. Some keep it simple with People, Companies and Deals or a deal-flow list. Others add LPs, Funds or Events objects. Common lists are Portfolio, VC Network and Founders Network.
- **Marketplaces:** Deals for partnerships with businesses, sometimes a Matching object to pair the two sides. At high volume, matching often happens in another tool.
- **Early-stage startups:** People, Companies and Deals, plus a Startup Fundraising list.
- **Recruiting and staffing:** People and Companies, plus a Recruiting list.

## Weigh the evidence

- The user's own words beat what you infer from their website. That means anything they tell you, their existing deal attributes, and their call notes, emails and descriptions of how the team works. When sources disagree, ask which is right rather than picking one.
- Useful signals on a website: the main button's wording, named customers and case studies, the pricing model and plans, partner pages, "For X" pages, and the job titles of the buyers it speaks to.
- Useful signals from the user: the things and pipelines they describe, "we want to track X", and anything that happens again and again.
- Treat gaps as questions for the user, never as facts.
